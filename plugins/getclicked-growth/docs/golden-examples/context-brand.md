# Brand Strategy

## Positioning Statement
For district SPED directors and school administrators who can't answer "is this 504 plan being followed?", Invincible is the school health communication platform that manages the plan in action — not the document, the daily reality — because every care note is logged in seconds, alerts route automatically, and the whole team sees the same timestamped timeline.

## Competitive Positioning
- **We are:** The execution layer for 504 plans and IEPs. The daily coordination tool between nurse, teacher, counselor, and parent.
- **We are NOT:** A compliance tool. Not a health records system. Not a replacement for Frontline or Magnus — we fill the gap they leave.
- **Primary differentiator:** We manage the plan in action. Competitors manage the plan document or the health record. Nobody else manages what happens every day between the plan and the people.
- **Against:** Frontline/Embrace/Go504 (they write the plan, we execute it), Magnus/SNAP (they store records, we coordinate care), email/text/paper (we replace the chaos)

## Voice & Tone

### Voice Attributes
| Attribute | What It Means | Example |
|-----------|--------------|---------|
| Clear | We say exactly what we mean in the fewest words possible. No jargon, no hedging. | "Log a care note in 30 seconds" not "Our intuitive interface streamlines the documentation process" |
| Grounded | We talk about real problems that real people have. We name specific roles, specific frustrations. | "When a parent calls the district office asking what happened today, you have to call the building" not "Communication challenges exist in educational settings" |
| Urgent | This isn't a nice-to-have. Every untracked accommodation is a compliance risk. Every unanswered parent is an escalation. | "Every unlogged care note is a liability" not "Consider improving your documentation practices" |

### Tone Calibration
| Context | Tone |
|---------|------|
| Website copy | Direct, specific, problem-first. Lead with the pain, then the relief. Show the product in action — screenshots, not illustrations. |
| Ad headlines | Ultra-specific to the persona. "Is your 504 plan being followed?" for district directors. "Parents expect coordinated care" for private school admins. |
| Blog/content | Educational and genuinely helpful. 504 plan guides that help parents and educators regardless of whether they use Invincible. |
| Social media | Founder-led. Bob's story. Behind-the-scenes of building for schools. Community of school nurses and SPED directors, not a corporate feed. |
| Email | Personal, from Bob. One clear purpose per email. No marketing fluff. |
| Conference talks | Story-first. "I was diagnosed with type 1 diabetes at 18. Here's what 100 families told me about the gap between the plan and the daily reality." |

### Forbidden Language
- Never use: "empower," "leverage," "stakeholder," "innovative solution," "cutting-edge," "digital transformation," "holistic," "best-in-class," "state-of-the-art"
- Never claim: "We're the leading platform for..." (unverifiable). "Trusted by thousands" (not yet true). Any outcome guarantee.
- Never sound: Corporate (no "we strive to provide"). Clinical (no "behavioral health management solution"). Salesy (no "limited time offer" or "act now").
- Never: Position as replacing people. Invincible helps care teams coordinate — it doesn't replace the nurse, the counselor, or the parent.

## Objection Handling

| Objection | Response Framework |
|-----------|-------------------|
| "We already have Frontline/Embrace for our 504s" | "Frontline manages the plan document — the forms, the workflows, the deadlines. Invincible manages the plan in action. We don't replace your compliance tool. We fill the gap between what the plan says and what actually happens daily. They write it. We make sure it's followed." |
| "Will teachers actually use it?" | "Logging a care note takes 30 seconds. No complex forms. No training sessions. If they can send a text, they can use Invincible. We built it by watching what school nurses actually do, not what IT departments think they should do." |
| "We can't afford another tool right now" | "Free tier: one care team, unlimited staff, no credit card. Start with your most complex student. If it works, expand. Small Teams is $1,500/year — less than one OCR complaint costs in staff time." |
| "Is it FERPA/HIPAA compliant?" | "Built for sensitive student health data from day one. FERPA and HIPAA compliant with role-based access controls and audit logging. This isn't a consumer app we retrofitted for schools." |
| "How is this different from just using email/text?" | "Email and texts work — until someone asks 'what happened last Tuesday?' and you're searching three inboxes, a group text, and a paper log. Invincible gives everyone one timeline. Timestamped, searchable, audit-ready. Nothing falls through the cracks." |

## Brand Guardrails
- Always lead with the problem, not the product. "Is your 504 plan being followed?" before "Invincible is a platform that..."
- Always name specific roles (SPED director, school nurse, 504 coordinator) — never generic "users" or "customers"
- Bob's founding story is an asset. Use it in conference talks, about pages, and first-touch content. Don't bury it.
- Personas are constraints. When writing for the District SPED Director, use her language ("compliance posture," "OCR," "parent escalation"). When writing for the Private School Admin, use his ("parent trust," "tuition justification," "substitute nurse continuity").
- Free tier is a feature, not a concession. Lead with it: "Start free. One care team. No credit card."
- Never compare directly to competitors by name in ads. In organic content and sales conversations, name them and explain the gap.
- Claims must be provable. "Founded from 100+ family interviews" is provable. "The best school health platform" is not.
