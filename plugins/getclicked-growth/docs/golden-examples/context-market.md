## Competitor Gaps

- **Manager enablement is unowned.** Every tool assigns tasks to the new hire. Nobody coaches the manager through the first 90 days. This is Bloom's sharpest wedge.
- **Time-to-productivity measurement doesn't exist.** Competitors track "onboarding task completion." Bloom tracks whether the new hire is actually ramping -- milestone completion, manager ratings, early performance signals.
- **Mid-market is underserved.** BambooHR is broadening upmarket. Enboarder starts at 1,000 employees. The 200-2,000 segment gets generic HRIS onboarding modules, not purpose-built tools.

---

## Industry Trends

- **Onboarding is a retention lever.** Companies with structured onboarding see 82% higher retention (Brandon Hall). HR leaders are shifting from "paperwork completion" to "experience design."
- **Manager quality is the #1 driver of early attrition.** Gallup data. Tools that coach managers (not just assign tasks) will win.
- **HRIS consolidation vs. best-of-breed.** Rippling and Deel are pulling everything into one platform. Counter-positioning: "Your HRIS handles records. Bloom handles the experience."

---

## Market Context

- **Addressable market:** ~45,000 US companies with 200-2,000 employees. At $8/emp/mo average, TAM is $2.4B.
- **Buying cycle:** Q1 budget planning, Q3 back-to-school hiring surges. HR tools purchased by VP People with CFO sign-off.
- **Key dynamic:** "We already have BambooHR" is the #1 objection. Answer: "BambooHR tracks paperwork. Bloom tracks whether your new hire is actually ramping."
